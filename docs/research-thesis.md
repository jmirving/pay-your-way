# Research thesis: where a software AI can pay its way now

## The constraint

The project should not pretend a language model can substitute for a materials lab, a civil engineer, or a licensed water-system operator. It should target loops where the science is mature, the intervention is measurable, and the limiting factor is analysis capacity, data friction, prioritization, or follow-through.

## Candidate areas considered

### 1. Small-system water loss and energy

Strong first target. The physical interventions are known: meter validation, active leakage control, faster repair, pressure management, and asset renewal. Water utilities already have established audit practice, but small systems often lack technical, managerial, and financial capacity. AI can reduce the cost of turning messy operational exports into an auditable decision packet and can keep the measurement loop alive month after month.

The initial product should complement established AWWA/EPA methods rather than replace them.

### 2. Wastewater aeration/process optimization

Potentially high energy impact, but direct control recommendations can affect permit compliance and treatment performance. This is a later track after the project has domain partners and a strong safety/verification framework.

### 3. Methane leak and landfill-gas prioritization

High climate leverage and increasingly good remote-sensing/public data. Promising second track, especially for identifying sites that deserve inspection. It still needs careful source attribution and on-site confirmation.

### 4. Carbon capture / direct-air-capture breakthrough generation

Important but not the first build. The bottleneck is not only idea generation; it is materials characterization, process integration, energy/cost performance, scale-up, and experimental validation. AI can help literature synthesis, experiment design, surrogate modeling, and candidate ranking, but a chat interface cannot complete the physical feedback loop.

### 5. Extraterrestrial water delivery

Not an operational water-security intervention. The orbital-energy, planetary-protection, impact-risk, capture, storage, and distribution problems dwarf the useful-water benefit. Keep it in the science-fiction folder.

## Product principle

Use AI where it is unusually good—schema interpretation, synthesis, anomaly explanation, planning, paperwork, and repeated follow-up—and keep physics/economics calculations deterministic and inspectable.

For every claimed intervention, track:

- baseline and measurement period
- input provenance and confidence
- physical quantity affected (water, energy, emissions)
- variable cost or avoided-cost basis
- counterfactual / comparison
- uncertainty and assumptions
- field verification required
- post-intervention measurement

The project's credibility should come from refusing to convert uncertainty into fake precision.
