# Primary research sources

This project should prefer primary/industry sources and should not silently turn guidance into proprietary reimplementations. The links below are starting points for design decisions and future validation.

## Water loss, small systems, and energy

- US EPA — Capacity Development / small public water systems: https://www.epa.gov/dwcapacity/learn-about-capacity-development
  - EPA reports that more than 97% of U.S. public water systems are small systems serving 10,000 or fewer people and highlights their technical, managerial, and financial capacity constraints.
- US EPA — 2026 Training and Technical Assistance for Small Systems Funding: https://www.epa.gov/dwcapacity/training-and-technical-assistance-small-systems-funding
  - Current federal spending demonstrates that expert/analyst capacity remains an active bottleneck for small and rural systems.
- US EPA — Energy Efficiency for Water Utilities: https://www.epa.gov/sustainable-water-infrastructure/energy-efficiency-water-utilities
  - Establishes the water-energy link and the importance of baseline energy use and pumping/treatment efficiency.
- US EPA — Control and Mitigation of Drinking Water Losses in Distribution Systems: https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P1009VCZ.TXT
  - Describes economic level of leakage, active leakage control, pressure management, repair, and asset management.

## Established water-audit ecosystem

- American Water Works Association — Free Water Audit Software v6.1: https://www.awwa.org/toolbox/free-water-audit-software/
  - Industry-standard top-down audit; v6.1 includes carbon calculations. Pay Your Way should complement this rather than replace it.
- American Water Works Association — Water Loss Control resources: https://www.awwa.org/resource/water-loss-control/
  - Includes the Audit Compiler and Leakage Component Analysis Model; important evidence that simple audit/trend tooling is already solved.
- Water Research Foundation — Project 5057, Level 1 Water Audit Validation Guidance Manual, Second Edition: https://www.waterrf.org/research/projects/level-1-water-audit-validation-guidance-manual-second-edition
  - Validation focuses on audit accuracy, methodology, and communicating uncertainty.
- Texas Water Development Board — Water Loss Dashboard: https://www.twdb.texas.gov/conservation/data/water-loss-dashboard.asp
  - Public validation program and reports provide a potential dataset for learning common audit-quality failure modes.
- California DWR — Urban Water Loss: https://water.ca.gov/Programs/Water-Use-And-Efficiency/Urban-Water-Use-Efficiency/Validated-Water-Loss-Reporting
  - Public validated audit ecosystem and another candidate data source.
- Georgia EPD — Water Efficiency and Water Loss Audits: https://epd.georgia.gov/watershed-protection-branch/water-efficiency-and-water-loss-audits
  - Publishes annual audit results and water-loss control guidance.

## AI in the water sector

- Water Research Foundation — Project 5290, The Emergence of AI in the Water Sector: Opportunities and Challenges for Water Resources Planning: https://www.waterrf.org/research/projects/emergence-ai-water-sector-opportunities-and-challenges-water-resources-planning-0
  - In-progress research on actionable AI implementation guidance for utilities; confirms that deployment/data-readiness, not merely model capability, is an active sector problem.

## Carbon removal context

- US DOE — Carbon Dioxide Removal: https://www.energy.gov/hgeo/carbon-dioxide-removal
- IEA — Financing CCUS at Scale, executive summary: https://www.iea.org/reports/financing-ccus-at-scale/executive-summary

These are retained as context for later climate tracks. Carbon-removal software may be valuable for literature synthesis, experiment design, candidate ranking, and techno-economic analysis, but a software-only project cannot close the required materials/process/scale-up validation loop by itself.
